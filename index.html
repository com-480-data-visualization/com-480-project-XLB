08

The Final Cut

What should the audience remember after the exploration?

Three things stuck with us after weeks staring at this data. Budget matters less than you'd think. Efficiency is everything. And some directors just keep winning.

"

Horror with no budget beats a Marvel film on ROI, almost every time. That was the most surprising thing we found.

— 45,697 films later
TAKE 01

Budget buys scale, not certainty.

Blockbusters make most of the money, yes. But they also miss break-even more often than you'd expect. Spending $200M is not a guarantee — it's a bet.

—
TAKE 02

Small films can punch absurdly high.

A $15K horror film can return more on investment than a $200M superhero movie. We checked. Multiple times.

—
TAKE 03

Franchises are compounding machines.

A great original becomes a franchise. A franchise becomes a brand. A brand can coast on nostalgia for a decade. Until it can't.

—
DATA & METHODOLOGY

Dataset spans 1880–2023, sourced from TMDb, IMDb and Box Office Mojo. Budget and revenue figures are nominal (not inflation-adjusted). ROI = revenue ÷ budget. Films with missing financials are excluded from monetary charts. Ratings are averaged user scores on a 1–5 scale.
